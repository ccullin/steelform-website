Mining vs Civil Construction Buckets: Key Differences in Design and Steel Grade

The bucket of an excavator is an important part of the machine’s productivity, longevity and efficiency when digging and moving material. While there are many buckets that seem to be alike when you look at them from afar, there are many differences between excavator buckets for civil construction jobs and those made for use in mining. These differences have to do with the way they are engineered, the grade of steel used to make them, the amount of wear protection on each bucket, and how each performs.

If you are a contractor, miner, or fleet manager and wish to prolong your equipment's life and minimise downtime, you need to know the differences between the buckets used for mining and civil construction jobs, how you can choose the right steel grade type, and why it is so important for Australia’s harsh mining environment to use a heavy-duty mining bucket.

Difference Between Mining and Civil Construction Buckets

The difference between mining excavator buckets and civil construction excavator buckets is based upon where the bucket will be used. The different conditions at the sites cause each type of bucket to have unique demands placed on it.

Civil construction buckets are generally intended for lighter duty and more versatile applications, including the following:

  • Soil excavation
  • Sand and gravel handling
  • Trenching
  • Site preparation
  • General earthmoving

Mining buckets are specially engineered for extremely heavy-duty applications such as the following:

  • Hard rock excavation
  • Iron ore and coal handling
  • Abrasive material loading
  • Heavy impact digging environments
  • Continuous high-output production cycles

Mining is a much harsher environment than civil construction, so mining buckets must be built with much higher levels of structural reinforcement and premium wear-resistant materials and have more advanced bucket designs than civil construction buckets.

Comparison of Rock vs General-Purpose Buckets

While there are many different types of excavator buckets, one of the biggest differences between rock buckets and general-purpose buckets is their design intention and the types of materials they are designed to dig.

  1. General Purpose Buckets

General-purpose buckets are designed for the majority of civil construction projects that consist of soft- to medium-density materials. The general-purpose buckets of a civil construction project will typically include handling of soil, landscaping, moving bulk materials and digging utility trenches.

This type of bucket is designed to provide the efficiency and versatility required for all general civil construction applications, with very little thought to their durability.

  1. Rock Buckets

Rock buckets are designed specifically for extreme abrasion and heavy-impact applications. Characteristics of a rock bucket include reinforced sidewalls, heavy-duty wear plates, higher-strength cutting edges, additional structural ribs and higher-grade steel construction.

Rock buckets are commonly used in quarrying and mining applications where bucket failure could result in significant loss of production and many hours of expensive downtime.

Steel Grade Matters: Excavator Bucket Steel Grade Differences

The type of steel used during the construction of an excavator bucket has a significant impact on the bucket’s ability to perform as intended. The steel grades used in buckets in civil projects and those used in mining projects are one of the main factors affecting the wear life, strength and anti-cracking ability of both types of buckets.

Steel Used in Buckets for Civil Construction

Civil construction buckets are manufactured with a standard type of high-strength structural steel that offers

  1. Good welding capabilities
  2. Moderate resistance to abrasion
  3. Cost-effective to manufacture
  4. Adequate durability for general-purpose use

These types of steel tend to perform well under conditions with low impact but will wear out faster in an environment that is highly abrasive.

Steel Used in Buckets for Mining

Mining buckets require premium-grade wear-resistant steel that is built to endure ongoing impact and abrasion while being able to

  1. Have high hardness ratings
  2. Have a high tensile strength
  3. Be resistant to cracking
  4. Have exceptional wear life

Many mining buckets use advanced wear plate materials, such as hardened alloy steel, in order to protect the area of the bucket where wear will occur at a high rate.

The difference in the types of materials used for constructing mining buckets and civil construction buckets is one of the primary differences in the various grades of steel used for constructing excavator buckets in the different industries.

Choosing the Right Bucket for Your Application

When choosing between a civil construction bucket and a mining bucket, there are numerous factors to consider:

  • Material Type: Soft soil has criteria for bucket specifications which differ from those of rock/ore (hard) or abrasive materials.
  • Operating Environment: Mining operating environments require more structural reinforcement and wear resistance than construction.

  • Excavator Size: The bucket weight and strength should be appropriate to the size of the excavator.

  • Productivity: Specially designed custom buckets for durability and maximum payload efficiency will help high-production operations.

Conclusion

Operators who understand the unique characteristics of the mining vs construction excavator buckets, including the variations in excavator bucket steel grades, will make an educated choice about which attachment is best for maximum durability and efficiency.
